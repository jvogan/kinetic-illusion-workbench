How the helix illusion works

  
Each particle keeps a fixed position on the rotating helix. On every frame, the renderer projects the particle, reads its signed view-space depth, and samples the cream-to-red-to-blue ramp. The viewer-aligned field changes the particle's displayed color as it rotates.


  
Why handedness can reverse

  
Orthographic projection removes the sign of depth. A right-handed helix and its depth-reflected left-handed mirror can produce the same projected positions when their rotation directions are opposite. With signed view-depth color, pixel equality also requires reversing the interpreted depth-color polarity. The Swap interpretation label control updates the description while preserving the canvas pixels.


  
Color rule

  
t = clamp(0.5 - polarity × view_z / (2 × R_depth), 0, 1)

  
R_depth is the maximum absolute view-space depth over every particle and a full revolution. The renderer keeps this scale fixed until the geometry or camera changes.


  
Acceptance test

  
Watch at least two complete turns. Try to perceive a right-handed helix rotating in one direction, then a left-handed helix rotating in the opposite direction. Use pixel comparison for geometric compatibility and viewer reports for perceptual reversal.
