01 · FINDING

The arrow wraps around a vertical cylinder

The reference video is 716 × 448 pixels at 30 frames per second. Its 365 frames contain two complete 180-frame revolutions plus five frames.

The measured boundaries fit an arrow-shaped strip wrapped around a vertical cylinder. The rounded renderer fit uses a 243 px radius, 54 px band height, 21.1° camera elevation, and orthographic projection. Its vertically shifted inner and outer boundaries match a cylindrical ribbon.

The red seam

The red seam occurs where the surface turns edge-on. Foreshortening compresses the persistent particles while the measured color ramp passes through orange and red.

Screen height also predicts much of the color field, with R² = 0.855, because the cylindrical projection couples depth and vertical position. Object height explains almost none of the color variation: R² = 0.015. The reconstruction therefore uses t = (sin(φ) + 1) / 2, with blue nearest, red near the tangents, and cream farthest.

03 · PERCEPTION

Depth reflection reverses the inferred rotation

Orthographic projection preserves image position but discards the sign of depth. Reflecting the model through the image plane preserves its projected movie. The reflection swaps the near and far assignments, so the inferred three-dimensional rotation reverses. Vision research calls this ambiguous structure from motion or kinetic depth.

The cream–red–blue field remains fixed to cylinder facing and depth while individual particles travel through it. This stationary field creates the red tangent seam as each material particle changes color.

VIEWING PROCEDURE

Test the ambiguity

  1. Watch at least two complete six-second loops.
  2. Focus on the upper arc, then focus on the lower arc.
  3. Add perspective or a near/far cue. Test whether the extra depth information stabilizes one reading.

04 · MEASUREMENT LIMITS

Measurements establish structure; viewers report perception

Geometry, optical flow, color, particle persistence, and exact frame recurrence can be measured directly. Those measurements distinguish a cylindrical ribbon from a flat ring and a view-depth color field from a screen-height gradient.

Use numerical analysis to establish the two geometric readings. Ask viewers whether the rotation reverses, how often it reverses, and which reading dominates.

06 · DNA/RNA DIRECTION

A helix couples handedness to rotation

Under orthographic projection, a rotating right-handed helix and its depth-reflected left-handed helix produce the same projected positions when their three-dimensional rotation signs are opposite. With signed view-depth color, pixel equality also requires reversing the interpreted depth-color polarity. Perceived handedness and perceived rotation direction can therefore reverse together. Viewer reports record whether the perception reverses spontaneously.

Preserve ambiguity

Use additive point rendering without depth sorting, perspective, shadows, fog, or depth-dependent particle size.

Prevent false sliding

Show both ends, limit the helix to three to five turns, and add one aperiodic marker such as a bulge or nick.

Handle color carefully

View-depth mode recolors particles from current view-space depth. Use the screen-height and constant-luma controls to measure how much the field biases handedness.

Geometry represented

The browser study represents B-DNA and A-form RNA as paired tubes with approximate pitch, rise, radius, and groove spacing.
